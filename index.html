Why CPA, not ROAS

I optimize for cost per acquisition.
ROAS is the side effect.

ROAS is a ratio. It looks great when you sell to people who were going to buy anyway, and it collapses the moment you try to grow. CPA is a cost. It tells you exactly what a new customer costs today, and whether you can afford more of them tomorrow.

01
ROAS rewards shrinking

The easiest way to raise ROAS is to spend less

Cut prospecting, retarget harder, and the ratio goes up while the business stays flat. A CPA target forces the only question that matters: how many customers can I buy this month at a price that still makes money?

02
CPA connects to the P&L

Margin minus CPA is profit per order

That is a number a founder can act on: raise budget, cut it, or fix the offer. A 4x ROAS says nothing about whether the 4 covers product cost, shipping and returns. A $26 CPA against a known margin does.

03
CPA is how Meta scales

Cost caps and bid caps speak CPA

Meta's bidding runs on cost per result, not on ROAS. When the account is built around a CPA target, the algorithm, the budget and the founder are all reading the same number, and spend can grow without the structure breaking. Astra Straps runs on $13–15 cost caps. Urban Air holds $48.33 across $5.5M.

04
ROAS still gets reported

I track it. I just don't chase it.

Blended ROAS, MER and net profit live in TripleWhale on every account, and they are on this page. But the target I set, test against and scale on is CPA by product and funnel stage. Hit that, and ROAS and profit take care of themselves.

How I set the target
Break-even CPA=AOV × gross margin
Target CPA=Break-even CPA − the profit you want per order

Everything under target is a signal to scale. Everything over it is a signal to fix the offer, the creative or the funnel stage, not to wait for a better ROAS.
